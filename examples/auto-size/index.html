  • 2010 — ImageNet arrives

    The ImageNet challenge put a million labelled photos online, and neural networks finally had enough data to start winning.

  • 2012 — The AlexNet moment

    A deep network trained on gaming GPUs won the ImageNet challenge by a wide margin, and the deep learning boom began almost overnight.

  • 2014 — Machines learn to imagine

    Generative adversarial networks pitted two models against each other, producing faces and pictures that nobody had programmed.

  • 2016 — AlphaGo defeats Lee Sedol

    DeepMind’s AlphaGo beat the world Go champion 4–1 in Seoul, mastering a game long thought too intuitive for machines.

  • 2017 — The transformer arrives

    The paper “Attention Is All You Need” introduced the transformer, the architecture every large language model still builds on.

  • 2020 — Scale changes everything

    GPT-3 reached 175 billion parameters and wrote passable prose, while AlphaFold predicted protein structures with near-laboratory accuracy.

  • 2022 — ChatGPT goes mainstream

    A free research preview reached a million users in five days and put generative AI in front of the general public for the first time.

  • 2023 — Frontier models, open weights

    GPT-4 went multimodal, Meta released Llama 2 for anyone to download, and EU lawmakers agreed the world’s first comprehensive AI act.

  • 2024 — Reasoning earns recognition

    Models learned to think step by step before answering, and the Nobel committee honoured neural-network pioneers in physics and chemistry.

  • 2026 — Agents become coworkers

    AI systems now plan, browse and execute multi-step work on their own, and the debate has shifted from what models know to what they should be allowed to do.
