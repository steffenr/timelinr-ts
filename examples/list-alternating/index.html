  • Watson takes the title

    IBM’s Watson beat the greatest Jeopardy! champions at their own quiz, and Siri shipped on the iPhone 4S that autumn — the first AI assistant millions of people carried in a pocket.

  • Words become geometry

    Word2vec turned vocabulary into coordinates, and meaning could suddenly be subtracted.

  • The industrial labs are founded

    OpenAI was announced with a billion-dollar pledge, Google opened TensorFlow to everyone, and the centre of gravity in machine learning moved from university departments to industry labs — a shift whose consequences, for better and for worse, are still being worked out a decade later.

  • Models learn to read

    BERT pushed pretrained language models into everyday search results, GPT-1 proved the recipe could scale, and the first export controls on AI accelerators appeared — the field had become geopolitically interesting.

  • Too dangerous to release

    OpenAI announced GPT-2 but withheld the weights, judging the full model too risky to publish; it then released them in staged increments over nine months. The argument that followed — about hype, hazard and who decides — was arguably the moment the public first took language models seriously.

  • Code writes itself

    GitHub Copilot began finishing whole functions from a comment, DALL-E drew what it was told, and diffusion models quietly overtook GANs as the standard way to generate an image.

  • Every image a prompt away

    Stable Diffusion was released openly in August, Midjourney’s Discord filled within weeks, and before the season was out “AI art” had gone from a research topic to a copyright scandal, a gallery prize controversy and a household phrase.

  • The policy year

    The European Parliament passed the AI Act, the White House answered with an executive order, and safety policies at the frontier labs turned from research notes into product features.

  • Agents report for work

    Systems that browse, file tickets and refactor repositories under supervision moved from demos to enterprise deployments, and “human in the loop” became a job description rather than a caveat.

  • Part of the plumbing

    The question is no longer whether a task can be automated, but which stack, whose weights and under what rules.
